# CBT Annotation Codebook (full rules)

This is the authoritative source for all 23 labels. Each entry has the project definition, **Yes if / No if** rules, and how to spot it. The project's definitions are narrow and sometimes differ from textbook CBT — follow these rules, not general knowledge.

## 1. Roles & data format

- **OP (Original Poster)** — the Reddit user who authored the post. Often also the **Seeker**.
- **Seeker** — the person requesting support.
- **Responder** — users other than the OP who reply. A responder can also be the OP.
- **Thread** — a series of consecutive direct comments; may involve multiple seekers/responders.
- Threads are rows in a Google Sheet, in thread order. First responder = blue; new users = new colors.
- **Gray text rows are NOT annotated — skip them.**

## 2. Focal Points

**RULE: one focal point per OP post, tagged at the Title.** Read the whole post, pick the single best of the three types, tag the title. Don't re-tag the same focal point in the body. Only add a new focal point where the conversation genuinely shifts to a new topic.

### Focal Point (Complaint)
The reason OP/responder presents to the group — venting, displeasure, fear/concern.
- **Yes if:** start of a new conversation or a topic shift; venting about something they're displeased about; expression of fear/concern. E.g. "Fuck this disease," "I'm terrified of being sucked into a black hole."
- **No if:** topic is still the same but just narrowed/elaborated.

### Focal Point (Request)
OP/responder presenting to ask for support — advice, help, info, resources, opinion.
- **Yes if:** asking for advice/help/information/resources/opinion. Any new question → tag. E.g. "My anxiety is debilitating—need advice," "Please help, what can I do…"
- If a "?" is missing, use context (bad grammar still counts).

### Focal Point (Statement)
A more neutral topic statement — personal story, update, review, recommendation-sharing.
- **Yes if:** personal story / neutral stance / accountability post. E.g. "I used to think therapy is fake but it actually helped me," "I posted before that I am depressed but now I am fine."

## 3. Psychoeducation
**Definition:** providing factual educational information explaining symptoms/behavior.
- **Yes if:** provides factual knowledge about a psychological/physiological/scientific topic to educate (acting like a scientist; "would I read this in a psych textbook?"). Must be about the human brain / how humans think. Must be a **neurobiology fact, not a theory**. Explaining the mechanism of how a therapy method works counts. Topics: medicine, disorders, symptoms, therapy.
- **No if:** factually incorrect (fact-check); not related to mind/emotions/mental processes; giving diet tips for ED; theorizing rather than stating a fact (→ Conceptualization); just a link with educational content.
- **Spot:** physiological/behavioral language — "cortisol," "dopamine," "sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight)."

## 4. Cognitive Distortion
**Definition:** internal thoughts lacking validity that increase anxiety/depressive symptoms; maladaptive, easily challenged.
- **Yes if:** describing an unrealistic reality; venting that is easily counterable/disproven; jumping to conclusions without evidence; hyperboles/extremes ("impossible," "absolutely no way").
- **No if:** seeker acknowledges their own distortion without someone else verbalizing it first (no journey to get there); statement uses "I feel like…" or "I'm probably…" → DON'T tag.
- **Spot:** "I will never be good enough," "Everyone hates me," "I mess up everything." Ask "Is this actually true?"

## 5. Restructuring
**Definition:** techniques to identify, evaluate, and correct inaccurate beliefs / dysfunctional schemas.
- **Yes if:** seeker changes perspective on a cognitive distortion (an "insertion of a different point of view"); responder helps seeker shift perspective ("take a step back"); responder suggests a restructuring technique. Often double-tagged with Recommendation.
- **No if:** seeker is introspecting rather than disproving a distortion (→ Reflection); responder theorizes the *why* without recommending an alternative perspective (→ Conceptualization).
- **Spot:** "I never thought about it that way before," "Put yourself in X's shoes."
- Don't double-tag with Recommendation unless it's actually a recommendation.

## 6. Reflection
**Definition:** engaging the self in attentive, critical, exploratory, iterative interaction with one's thoughts/actions, with a view to changing them.
- **Yes if:** seeker examines own thoughts/feelings/actions for deeper understanding (in post or throughout body); responder encourages the seeker to examine theirs; **self-awareness as a result of discussion (always required)**; indicates they'll reexamine/look at behavior in a new light / had a revelation after a discussion.
- **No if:** shifting perspective on a cognitive distortion (→ Restructuring); not introspective.
- **Spot:** "I" statements — "I never thought about it that way," "This really opened my eyes," "Looking back, I shouldn't have said that." Annotate redundantly across the conversation where reflection happens.

## 7. Summarizing
**Definition:** restating another person's post. **More than one idea needed to tag.**
- **Yes if:** responder brings up main points of seeker's post and restates in own words ("so you're feeling…"); reiterates a self-disclosure; presents as confirmation they heard the seeker; uses summarizing to direct the seeker to a pain point. Can co-occur with Validation.
- **No if:** seeker self-summarizes; responder uses seeker's point to talk about themselves (jumping-off point); responder adds their own opinion in the summary.

## 8. Cognitive Conceptualization
**Definition:** giving a concept theoretical meaning — a hypothesis "X causes Y."
- X = behavior/thought/cognition; Y = why X. Can you fill in "[psychological thing] happens because [reason]"? Is X about someone's mind/emotions/behavior? Is Y explaining *why* X exists, not just what happened next?
- **Yes if:** theorizing a behavior/thought/cognition (anyone's); hypothesizing a feeling is caused by something with a mechanism; identifying a cause/effect of a psychological state to describe how it came to be. Can be about one's own.
- **No if:** statement without explanation ("you stink" vs "you stink because you haven't showered"); has X but not Y or vice versa ("I'm nervous in public" = X, no Y); X not about a person's behavior/feelings/psychological state; it's a **fact not a theory** (anything about an emotion/feeling is a theory; pattern recognition is a fact and doesn't count); justification rather than theorizing; only argues why something *doesn't* explain a behavior without giving an alternative.
- **Spot:** any statement with reasoning, good or bad. Look for the word **"because."**
- **Borderline — questions:** when the X-because-Y reasoning is framed as a *question directed at someone* ("so were you compensating for the food the whole time?") rather than an asserted theory, it's borderline. Don't confidently tag — flag it for team discussion. An asserted theory ("she does X because Y") is a cleaner tag than an interrogative probe.
- **Autobiographical causal clauses (SD vs SD + Conceptualization):** a "because"/"due to" clause inside the OP recounting their own life is **Self-Disclosure**. It *also* earns Conceptualization only when it theorizes the **origin or mechanism of a psychological state or a sustained behavior pattern** — "how that state came to be" — not when it just attaches an immediate trigger to a current symptom being disclosed. Worked pair from this project: "I have been binge eating lately due to stress" → **Self-Disclosure only** (current symptom + proximate trigger, folded into the symptom report). "What kept me from getting professional help was my pediatrician [who dismissed me at 16]" → **Self-Disclosure + Conceptualization** (theorizes the origin of a decades-long help-avoidance pattern). The two labels co-occur; adding Conceptualization never removes the Self-Disclosure.

## 9. Clinical Referral
**Definition:** directing someone to a professional/clinical source of treatment. **Double-tag with Recommendation.**
- **Yes if:** recommending professional medical help (implicit or explicit); can span multiple lines (tag them all, especially when implicit); recommending a different kind of professional help than one already used; recommending talking to a doctor already seen counts.
- **No if:** recommending something that is NOT professional medical help (→ Recommendation).
- **Spot:** clinical terminology; mentions of a medical professional.

## 10. Recommendation
**Definition:** suggesting a practice/resource for therapeutic intervention.
- **Yes if:** suggests something helpful **for the first time**; adds additional info (tag too); responds specifically to seeker's request for recommendations (implicit rec); if another label applies (e.g. Clinical Referral), also label Recommendation; an exchange providing additional info between repeated recommendations.
- **No if:** suggests something unhelpful/harmful/incorrect (→ Bad Advice); states something that worked for them *without suggesting it*; no implicit recommendations unless Focal Point (Request); no action words + no new info (just re-emphasizing a prior rec); not between seeker & receiver. **A line about the speaker's own habit/experience that follows a recommendation (e.g. "I watch her videos a lot, she's level headed") is Self-Disclosure, not added-info Recommendation** — "adds additional info" means new actionable detail about the recommended thing, not the speaker disclosing their own relationship to it.
- **Spot:** "try," "look into," "think about." If it's bad advice, don't tag Recommendation here — tag Bad Advice (which itself double-tags Recommendation).

## 11. Journal / Thought Record
**Definition:** recording thoughts/moods/emotions (journaling). **Double-tag with Recommendation when recommended. Tag at first instance in response (like Validation).**
- **Yes if:** recommends writing down/tracking thoughts and feelings; discusses journaling but doesn't recommend it; drawing counts (for recording thoughts/emotions); verbal/audio reflection recorded individually for reflective purposes (documentation).
- **No if:** heart-rate variability or other non-psychological data; verbal/audio recording that is a conversation, not reflection.

## 12. Grounding Technique
**Definition:** strategies to become aware of the here-and-now; to reverse an adverse physiological response.
- **Yes if:** collecting yourself / calming down to reverse an adverse physiological response (54321, progressive muscle relaxation, box/square breathing); using music to calm sensations/emotions back to baseline.
- **No if:** no strategy recommended/disclosed; the technique is used while NOT in a negative state (not for recovery). Example: journaling everyday = false; journaling during an anxiety attack = true. The method must move from a negative state toward neutral/positive.
- **Spot:** breathing techniques, meditation, listening to music, physical relaxation.

## 13. Non-Expert Diagnosis
**Definition:** a Redditor stating another (or themselves) has a specific diagnosis, with exact diagnosis terminology. No one in the forum has authority to diagnose.
- **Yes if:** seeker/responder asserts a diagnosis for self or interlocutor ("You are definitely schizophrenic"); stated **without doubt**; may be more than one; if self-disclosed, assume real unless evidence they diagnosed without professional insight.
- **No if:** professionally diagnosed (don't tag); hedging ("you might have X / I think X but I'm not sure"); any doubt ("Don't quote me, but…"); any probability word ("highly likely," "it's possible"); questions don't count. "I'm not an expert but you have X" → do not label.
- **Spot:** diagnostic language — depression, anxiety, bipolar, schizophrenic, antisocial. "Innocent until proven guilty."

## 14. Incorrect Information
**Definition:** statements carrying factually/statistically inaccurate information. **Can co-occur with Bad Advice — tag both.** Only needs 1 wrong thing.
- **Yes if:** blatantly wrong advice/"knowledge" (when in doubt, Google it); incorrect psychoeducation.
- **No if:** statement is correct/researched/statistically accurate; an opinion/implicit recommendation that is statistically inaccurate (→ Bad Advice).
- **Spot:** easy ("lotion causes bone cancer") or subtle (calling introversion "antisocial" — Antisocial Personality Disorder is different).

## 15. Bad Advice
**Definition:** maladaptive/harmful advice to remedy symptoms. **Must be a recommendation (implicit or explicit) — double-tag with Recommendation.**
- **Yes if:** a recommendation/advice that is maladaptive, unhealthy, unsafe, or statistically inaccurate for the condition/symptom/diagnosis; implicit bad advice counts; if a responder gives incorrect information AND a recommendation → also Bad Advice.
- **No if:** the statement is correct and safe to follow / researched / statistically accurate.
- **Spot:** recommendation obviously non-beneficial. Must be **actionable** — insults don't count.

## 16. Self-Disclosure
**Definition:** revealing information about oneself to contribute to conversation/rapport.
- **Yes if:** "I" statement + feelings/needs/fears/hopes; personal history (events, symptoms, relationships); identity markers (demographics, roles, affiliations); beliefs as identity/values ("As a Buddhist, I…"); disclosing an experience they had (not reading about something).
- **No if:** a simple opinion rather than a strong belief ("I think cocaine smells better than alcohol"); "me too"; preferences; trolling.
- **Spot:** sharing personal details without stealing the spotlight or goading the OP. Annotate cell by cell.
- **Autobiographical-block breadth:** inside an OP's personal narrative, tag Self-Disclosure cell-by-cell on every line that recounts the OP's own lived experience — **including lines whose grammatical subject is someone else** within that experience ("he patted me on the back," "my mom cried the entire drive home"). These are still the OP disclosing *their* experience. Leave blank only (a) lines that merely relay another person's stated opinion/claim as content (e.g. the therapist's view that weight naturally fluctuates with age), and (b) bare context with no personal content (e.g. "this was in 2002"). The test is **recounting-own-experience (tag) vs relaying-someone-else's-opinion or bare-fact (skip)** — not who the sentence's grammatical subject is.

## 17. Social Support
**Definition:** emotional support, belonging, being valued, practical help, info/guidance — via social connection.
- **Yes if:** a recommendation (implicit "[] did this and it helped" or explicit) to spend time around other humans; offering to private-message as support; recounting success dealing with a problem through time with others; recommending online support groups for support; recommending speaking with a trusted authority figure.
- **No if:** mentioning someone else as a statement with no/negative effect on mental health; recommending time with a non-human (e.g. AI girlfriend).
- **Spot:** any social structure — family, friends, classmates, internet friends, public space.

## 18. Confirmation Bias
**Definition:** seeking/interpreting info consistent with existing beliefs, ignoring inconsistent info.
- **Yes if:** there's a cognitive distortion (jump in logic) beforehand leading to the thought; seeker/responder tries to justify/confirm/validate their distortion with **bad evidence**; must refer to an actual experience.
- **No if:** cherry-picking partial truths WITHOUT a previously expressed distortion (ragebaiting themselves); simply restating a distortion without new info (no example/anecdote).
- **The prior distortion must be explicitly verbalized in the text — do not infer it from affect.** Pride, excitement, or tone alone ("I gloated and was beaming with pride") is not a stated distortion. If the only evidence a distortion exists is the speaker's emotional state, do NOT tag Confirmation Bias — tag Self-Disclosure for the affect and move on. Worked No-example from this project: "I was excited that I could continue on this path since I was told I was just fine" → **Self-Disclosure only**, not Confirmation Bias (the underlying pro-ED belief is implied by affect but never asserted).
- **Spot:** evidence that supports a prior cognitive distortion (e.g. anxious Redditor sees a black cat → catastrophe sign).

## 19. Validation
**Definition:** recognizing another's emotions/thoughts/behaviors as understandable given their circumstance. **Tag like focal point — first instance in the text block only.**
- **Yes if:** responder shares a similar experience to normalize the seeker's; empathizes ("I'm so sorry," putting themselves in seeker's shoes); confirms seeker's emotions/opinion are reasonable / says they understand.
- **No if:** self-disclosing something irrelevant to the seeker's post; confirming a "correct" choice (not psychological — must be about emotions); simply agreeing with a **factual/non-emotional** statement ("Sleep is the best thing ever" → "You're so right!"); a general validating statement to no one in particular.
- **Note on agreement:** agreeing with an *emotional complaint* DOES count as Validation (e.g. "Fucking AGREED." in response to an emotional vent confirms the seeker's feeling is reasonable). But a bare "Agreed." attached to a *content critique or opinion* (no direct emotional object being affirmed) is "simply agreeing" → do NOT tag. The test: is the agreement affirming someone's *emotional experience/feeling*, or just endorsing a factual/evaluative opinion? Only the former is Validation.
- **Spot:** "Understandable," "Makes sense," "That's terrible," "It makes sense you're feeling X because you went through Y."

## 20. Encouraging Self-Compassion
**Definition:** being supportive toward oneself when suffering. **Double-tag with Recommendation.**
- **Yes if:** responder recommends the seeker be kinder to themselves (like talking to a loved one; can be actions — "take care of yourself," recommending self-care); a specific recommendation for the seeker.
- **No if:** not a recommendation; responder is just being compassionate themselves.
- **Spot:** "you should learn to be nicer to yourself," "Love yourself." Compare self-talk to how you'd talk to a loved one.

## 21. Goal Setting
**Definition:** setting a concrete goal; goal consensus. The goal can be set by seeker or responder.
- **Yes if:** it is **SMART** — specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, time-bound (**must be at least 4 of 5**).
- **No if:** a simple recommendation without much specificity.
- **Spot:** a concrete SMART goal set by a recommender or the person themselves — NOT a goal talked about in general.

## 22. Body Mindfulness
**Definition:** conscious awareness of the body / physiological reactions, not only emotional response.
- **Yes if:** seeker mentions a physical sensation somewhere in the body as a manifestation of their condition/emotions (heart rate counts); responder encourages the seeker to be aware of physical manifestations of emotions ("notice sensations as they come to you").
- **No if:** neurological/cognitive ("my brain shuts down"); could be explained by an Inside Out character (personified emotion).
- **Spot:** "My [diagnosis/complaint] makes me feel [physical symptom]."

## 23. Acceptance
**Definition:** opening up to / making peace with / living with private events without trying to remove or control them.
- **Yes if:** seeker/responder comes to terms with a fact about themselves/their condition **over the course of a conversation** (can be an unfortunate reality); responder recommends the seeker accept something about themselves; if seeker responds positively ("You're right"), tag that line too.
- **No if:** acknowledging problems on their own without a "journey" to the truth; surrendering to an existing distortion/incorrect belief ("accepting" you'll die alone because you're unlovable).
- **Spot:** "let it go," "come to terms with it," "make peace," "learn to live with it," "accept it." Acceptance must come as an effect after a conversation, unless it's a recommendation.

## 24. Gratitude
**Definition:** expressing thanks.
- **Yes if:** seeker expresses genuine gratitude after receiving a helpful response.
- **No if:** sarcasm; preemptive gratitude (in advance) before anyone helped (e.g. in the original post).
- **Spot:** "thank you," "you helped," "that really helped" — as a result of a conversation.

## 25. Double-tag summary (enforce automatically)

| Primary label | Auto-add | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical Referral | Recommendation | always |
| Bad Advice | Recommendation | always (Bad Advice must be a rec) |
| Encouraging Self-Compassion | Recommendation | when it's a rec to the seeker |
| Journal / Thought Record | Recommendation | when recommended |
| Restructuring | Recommendation | only when a technique/action is recommended |
| Incorrect Information | Bad Advice | when the wrong info is also harmful actionable advice |

**First-instance-only labels:** Focal Point (at title), Validation (first instance in block), Journal/Thought Record (first instance in response).
